The pattern is consistent — but not enough to flip the strategy. Survivors clustered where you'd expect quality to hide: liquidity (>$100M/day traded beat SPY 42% of the time vs 14% for the illiquid microcaps), shallower declines (down 75–80% beat SPY 23% vs 10% for the down-90%+ wreckage), and smaller-but-not-tiny caps ($300M–1B beat SPY 26% vs 11% for >$5B). Waiting for the price to reclaim its 200-day average helped too, but only 119 entries ever qualified. Even the best-characterised subgroup still beat the index less than half the time — the takeaway is a filter to avoid the worst (illiquid, down-90%+, high-vol names), not a recipe that turns the screen into a winner.
Read this before trusting the numbers

Survivorship bias — the big one

The universe is today's listed US common stocks. A company that fell 75%+ and later went bankrupt or was taken under is largely absent from the data, so the surviving winners are over-represented. In this run the delisted-adjustment caught 0 mid-window delistings out of 8,738 positions — effectively zero — which is itself the tell: the dataset is built from names that are still listed today, so the losers that went to zero were never in it to begin with. That makes the "Delisted-adjusted" column nearly identical to the raw one and means even these grim numbers are an optimistic ceiling, not a floor.

Other limitations

  • Market cap at each historical date is estimated as current cap × (price then ÷ price now), assuming constant share count. Buybacks and dilution distort this; delisted names fall back to a $2M median dollar-volume liquidity filter.
  • Prices are split/dividend-adjusted (Yahoo auto-adjust). No transaction costs, slippage, or bid-ask spread — real small-cap execution is worse.
  • Equal-weighted, no position sizing or stop-losses.
  • The 100-point fundamental scorer is not replayed. It reads yfinance .info (short interest, analyst ratings, P/S, P/B) and financial statements, which are only served as today's snapshot — scoring a 2019 cohort with 2026 fundamentals would be lookahead bias. The "what characterized the winners" analysis therefore uses only point-in-time price/volume features that were genuinely observable on each entry date. A faithful fundamental replay is possible but needs point-in-time data (EDGAR XBRL for statements, FINRA for short interest) — a larger build.
  • Overlapping cohorts share market regimes, so monthly results are not independent; treat per-year medians as the honest unit.
