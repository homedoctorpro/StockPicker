Survivorship bias — the big one

The universe is today's listed US common stocks. A company that fell 75%+ and later went bankrupt or was taken under is largely absent from the data, so the surviving winners are over-represented. In this run the delisted-adjustment caught 0 mid-window delistings out of 8,738 positions — effectively zero — which is itself the tell: the dataset is built from names that are still listed today, so the losers that went to zero were never in it to begin with. That makes the "Delisted-adjusted" column nearly identical to the raw one and means even these grim numbers are an optimistic ceiling, not a floor.

Other limitations

  • Market cap at each historical date is estimated as current cap × (price then ÷ price now), assuming constant share count. Buybacks and dilution distort this; delisted names fall back to a $2M median dollar-volume liquidity filter.
  • Prices are split/dividend-adjusted (Yahoo auto-adjust). No transaction costs, slippage, or bid-ask spread — real small-cap execution is worse.
  • Equal-weighted, no position sizing or stop-losses. The disqualifier and 100-point scorer from the live report are not applied here — this tests the raw price screen only.
  • Overlapping cohorts share market regimes, so monthly results are not independent; treat per-year medians as the honest unit.
